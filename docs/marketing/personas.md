# Personas — Kamoe Bisa

Four people the product is genuinely built for, each mapping to a capability
that already exists. Written 2026-08-05 alongside `positioning.md`; read that
first, since the market category decision drives everything here.

> **⭐ PRIMARY PERSONA CHANGED 2026-08-16.** It was Marijke, the heritage
> learner (§2). It is now Robin, the partner (§1) — owner decision, taken while
> working on the landing-page rewrite. Marijke's motive is retrospective: she is
> reconnecting with a family past. Robin's is happening this weekend, at a table
> where everyone switches to Dutch out of politeness. Same language pair, more
> urgency, and a live-in native speaker changes which features matter.
>
> Everything downstream follows from this: `positioning.md` §4, the landing-page
> hero, and `docs/plans/2026-08-16-landing-page-redesign.md`. Marijke is NOT
> deleted — she is still real and still served, and the loanword bridge is still
> the first thing both of them see.

**Health warning, stated once and meant:** these are *hypotheses*. Dunford's
first positioning step is to understand the customers who already love your
product, and there are none yet — one test account, zero paying subscribers.
Everything below is inferred from the feature set, the owner's own motivation
and the market. The first ten real customers should either confirm or destroy
these, and this file gets rewritten from what they say. Do not let confident
prose here harden into assumed fact.

## 1. Robin — the partner  ⭐ PRIMARY

**Owner decision 2026-08-16: the landing page speaks to Robin first.**

30s–40s, Dutch. Their partner is Indonesian. The in-laws are alive and present —
weekly video calls, visits, a wedding at some point. At family gatherings
everyone switches to Dutch or English out of politeness, and Robin can feel the
gap that courtesy creates. Their partner talks to Jakarta on the phone most
weeks and Robin catches a handful of words.

**The job they are hiring the product for:** not fluency, and definitely not a
holiday. They want to stop being the person the room accommodates — to greet
their mother-in-law properly, follow the thread at dinner, and say something
back without their partner having to relay it.

**Why this one is primary.** It is the owner's own path, which is the reason the
landing page can drop the marketing voice and tell it in the first person (see
`docs/plans/2026-08-16-landing-page-redesign.md`). It also carries more urgency
than Marijke's: the family occasions are already in the calendar.

**What they have already tried:** Duolingo — which does not offer Indonesian to
Dutch speakers at all, so they either went through English or bounced. Asking
their partner, which fails the way it always does: intimate relationships make
poor classrooms, correction reads as criticism, and both sides quietly stop.

**What lands, and it is different from Marijke:**
- **Spreektaal is central, not a later track.** What Robin hears at home is
  *capek*, *banget*, *duit*, *bentar* — not *lelah*, *sekali*, *uang*,
  *sebentar*. A course that teaches only *bahasa baku* trains them for a
  register their family does not use. 66 register pairs are live.
- **The loanword bridge still works**, but reframed: not "your grandmother's
  world" — "you already know more of your partner's language than you think".
- **A permanent practice partner.** Unlike Marijke, whose "ask the
  daughter-in-law" lasted two evenings, Robin lives with a native speaker. The
  product's job is to make them ready to use that, not to replace it.

**Objections to answer:** "I'm surrounded by it and still can't speak — what
will be different this time?" · "I don't have an hour a day." · "Will I sound
ridiculous?" Answer all three with *ten minutes a day, and you are not starting
from zero.*

**⚠️ Honesty limit, and it binds the copy.** The coursebook dialogues are formal
and touristy — lesson 2 is a Dutch businessman checking into a hotel. Spreektaal
rides alongside the lessons; it is not a family-conversation course. The page may
promise *you will understand the register they actually use* — true, shipped,
66 pairs — but never *chat with your in-laws by week two*. It is a structured
A1→B1 climb.

**Where they are:** mixed-nationality couple communities, Indonesian diaspora
groups in NL, r/indonesia, wedding and visa forums. Partly searchable, partly
community — less purely word-of-mouth than Marijke.

**Willingness to pay:** high, and sooner than Marijke. The motivation renews
every time the family visits.

**Their Dutch children — deferred, not dismissed.** The owner raised the couple's
children as a possible extension on 2026-08-16 and then set it aside: *"lets
focus on getting it right for the adult first"*. It has **no product behind it**
— the content is an adult Leiden coursebook, CEFR-levelled, so a teenager could
use it and a seven-year-old could not, and `entitlements` is one row per user
with two single-seat Stripe prices. Promising family learning would need seats
and probably younger content BEFORE the copy could honestly say it.

## 2. Marijke — the heritage learner  *(was primary until 2026-08-16)*

**Superseded as the landing-page lead by Robin (§1) — still real, still served.**
Her hook, the loanword wall, survives the change intact and is the first thing
both personas meet.

58, Dutch. Her grandmother was born in Bandung and came over in the fifties.
Growing up there was *ketjap* on the table, *pasar malam* every summer, an oma
who slipped into a language nobody translated. She has an Indonesian
daughter-in-law and two grandchildren who giggle in a language she cannot
follow.

**The job she is hiring the product for:** not fluency, and definitely not a
certificate. She wants to say something real to her in-laws and understand the
reply. She wants the family part of herself to stop being a set of food words.

**What she has already tried:** Duolingo — where Indonesian is not offered in
Dutch at all, so she either did it through English or bounced. Asking her
daughter-in-law, which lasted two evenings and made them both uncomfortable. A
phrasebook before a trip in 2019.

**Why she stopped:** everything on offer treats her as a beginner tourist, when
in fact she already recognises a hundred words and carries an emotional stake no
tourist has.

**What lands:** the loanword wall. *Kantoor → kantor.* Recognition, not
instruction. Then spreektaal, because family talk is not *bahasa baku*.

**Objections to answer:** "I'm too old to learn a language." "I tried before and
failed." "Is this another app I'll abandon?" — answer all three with *you are
not starting from zero, and 10 minutes a day is the whole commitment.*

**Where she is:** Indische and Moluccan community organisations, *tempo doeloe*
Facebook groups, pasar malam events, family WhatsApp. **Reached by word of mouth
and community, not by search.**

**Willingness to pay:** high in principle, low in confidence. She would happily
spend €254.50 on a Volksuniversiteit course if she believed it would work —
that is the real alternative, and it is 4.5× the current annual price.

## 3. Thijs — the traveller / soon-to-be expat

41, going to Indonesia for three months, or already living in Bali or Jakarta
on a remote contract. Wants to be understood at a *warung*, negotiate a
*kost*, talk to colleagues without switching to English.

**Job:** functional competence, fast, in the register people actually use.

**Already tried:** a phrasebook; Duolingo in English; asking Indonesian
colleagues who politely switch back to English.

**What lands: spreektaal.** He has already been embarrassed by textbook forms —
using *Anda* where everyone says *kamu*. The register toggle is exactly his
problem, and no competitor in this pair offers it.

**Objections:** "Will I actually be understood?" "Is this too slow/academic?"

**Where he is:** expat forums and Facebook groups, r/indonesia, digital-nomad
communities, Bali/Jakarta relocation content. **Reachable by search** — he types
things like "leer Indonesisch snel".

**Willingness to pay: highest of the three, and soonest.** He has a deadline and
a budget. Would not blink at €12/month for three months.

## 4. Sanne — the stalled app-hopper

29, finished everything Duolingo has (in English), has a few hundred words and
no grammar. Cannot form a sentence she has not memorised. Feels stuck and
slightly cheated.

**Job:** break the plateau. Understand *why* Indonesian works the way it does.

**What lands:** the placement test (`/instaptoets`) — do not make her start at
"hello" — and then the affix trainer, because affixes are exactly the wall she
hit. Duolingo gave her vocabulary and no morphology.

**Objections:** "Will this just repeat what I know?" (the placement test *is* the
answer) and "how is this different from the app I already quit?"

**Where she is:** actively searching. r/languagelearning, "Duolingo alternative",
"Indonesian grammar explained". **The most SEO-reachable of the three.**

**Willingness to pay:** moderate, and comparison-shops against Duolingo Super
(€122.99/yr). Price-anchored to apps, not to courses.

## How the four change what we build

| | **Robin ⭐** | Marijke | Thijs | Sanne |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First screen | spreektaal + loanword wall | loanword wall | spreektaal | placement test |
| Hook | being included at the table | recognition | being understood | respect for prior knowledge |
| Channel | couple/diaspora communities + search | community, word of mouth | search + expat forums | search |
| Urgency | high, recurring (every family visit) | low, emotional | high, deadline | medium, frustration |
| Price anchor | €254.50 course | €254.50 course | €12/mo, 3 months | €122.99/yr Duolingo |

Note how close Robin and Thijs are on *what lands* — both need spreektaal, and
for the same reason: the register people actually speak. They differ on motive
(belonging vs transacting) and on how long the motivation lasts. Robin's renews;
Thijs's expires with the trip.

Four different first screens is a real argument for the landing page carrying
more than one entry point — a hero for Robin, and clearly-labelled doors for the
others rather than a single generic funnel.

## What would falsify these

- **If the first paying customers are mostly Marijke rather than Robin, revert
  the 2026-08-16 change** — the hero goes back to heritage and the partner story
  becomes a secondary door. This is the newest and least-tested claim in the file.
- If they are overwhelmingly Thijs, the landing page leads with spreektaal alone
  and the loanword bridge moves down.
- If nobody uses `/instaptoets`, Sanne is not real and the affix trainer is a
  retention feature rather than an acquisition one.
- If community channels outperform search 5:1, the whole content plan is wrong
  and budget goes to events and partnerships instead.

Instrument before assuming: signup source, first surface used, and whether the
placement test is taken, are the three measurements that decide this.

⚠️ Robin is inferred from the owner's own experience, which is the strongest
reason to trust the *motive* and the weakest reason to trust its *size*. A
market of one you know intimately is easy to mistake for a market. Nothing here
says how many Dutch people with Indonesian partners will pay €79 a year, and
`docs/research/2026-07-06-market-research-competitive-landscape.md` §2 sizes the
heritage segment (~1.5–2M) but not this one. Size it before spending on channels
aimed at it.
