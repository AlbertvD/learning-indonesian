# Acquisition channels — Bullseye

Framework: Weinberg & Mares, *Traction*. Their central claim, quoting Thiel:
*"It is very likely that one channel is optimal. Most businesses actually get
zero distribution channels to work. Poor distribution — not product — is the
number one cause of failure."*

Bullseye is five steps: **brainstorm → rank → prioritise → test → focus**. The
discipline that matters is brainstorming *every* channel before ranking, because
we all arrive with biases. Ours is obvious and worth naming: this is a software
project, so the instinct is SEO and content. That instinct may be wrong for the
primary persona.

---

## The ranking

Against the 19 channels, ranked for **Marijke, the heritage learner** (primary).

### Inner ring — test these three first

**1. Community building.** Marijke is not a market segment, she is a community:
Indische and Moluccan organisations, *tempo doeloe* Facebook groups, pasar malam
events, heritage associations, the Moluccan community in Vught/Assen/Capelle.
These groups have newsletters, committees and event calendars, and they are
actively interested in keeping the language alive. This is the highest-fit
channel and the least like a marketing campaign.
*Cheap test:* approach three organisations offering a free workshop or a talk
("de Nederlandse woorden in het Indonesisch"). Measure replies, not signups.

**2. Targeting blogs / niche press.** A handful of Dutch outlets and blogs cover
Indisch heritage, Indonesian travel and language learning. Small audiences,
extremely high fit. Includes podcasts on Indisch history and Indonesia.
*Cheap test:* pitch the loanword story — *"173 Nederlandse woorden die je al in
het Indonesisch kent"* — to five outlets. It is a genuinely good story
independent of the product, which is what makes it placeable.

**3. Content marketing / SEO.** Real but slower, and it primarily reaches Sanne
and Thijs rather than Marijke. Justified by the fact that the corpus already
exists (affixes, grammar patterns, loanwords, stories) and by there being no
Dutch-language competition for these queries at all.
*Cheap test:* publish the public loanword page and three affix explainers,
measure impressions in Search Console over six weeks.

### Middle ring — plausible, not first

- **Viral marketing.** The loanword list is genuinely shareable — "did you know
  *kulkas* is *koelkast*?" is a WhatsApp-family-group message. Not a strategy on
  its own, but the *product* should be built so sharing is easy. Cheap to
  support, so do it opportunistically.
- **Public relations.** The founder story (Dutch-Indonesian family, built the
  thing he wanted) is a real story, and regional press likes local-founder
  angles.
- **Existing platforms.** Reddit r/languagelearning, r/indonesia; Facebook
  groups. Low cost, moderate fit, easy to get wrong by being spammy.
- **Speaking engagements.** Heritage association meetings, library talks. High
  fit with Marijke's demographic, poor scalability, but excellent for the
  customer-development conversations the pricing work needs.

### Outer ring — deprioritise for now

SEM (tiny search volume in Dutch for this pair, though cheap to test later),
social & display ads (no brand, weak targeting for heritage), offline ads,
email marketing (no list yet — becomes relevant once there is one), engineering
as marketing (the loanword page arguably *is* this), business development,
sales, affiliate programs, trade shows, offline events, unconventional PR.

Email is worth flagging as **deferred, not dismissed**: once there are a few
hundred learners, it is usually one of the strongest retention channels, and
retention is where a €7–9/month business actually lives.

## Why the ordering runs against instinct

The temptation is to start with SEO because it is the one we can do alone, at a
keyboard, without talking to anyone. Weinberg's warning is precisely this:
founders pick channels they are comfortable with rather than channels their
customers are in. Marijke does not search for "leer Indonesisch" — she has not
thought of herself as a language learner. She hears about it from someone in her
community.

So: community and press first, content in parallel because the corpus is already
built and the marginal cost is low.

## What to measure

Per *Traction*'s testing chapter, each test needs a number decided in advance.

| Test | Metric | Threshold to pursue |
|---|---|---|
| Three heritage organisations | replies, and whether any offers a slot | ≥1 concrete opportunity |
| Five blog/podcast pitches | placements | ≥1 placement |
| Loanword page + 3 explainers | Search Console impressions at 6 weeks | ≥500/month and rising |

None of these needs budget. All of them need a person, which is the actual
constraint.

## The measurement gap that blocks all of this

There is **no analytics on the public site** — no signup-source attribution, no
event tracking. Every conclusion above would currently be unfalsifiable.

Note the constraint this must respect: the privacy policy states there are no
analytics or tracking tools and no third-party cookies, and that claim is now
pinned by a test. Any measurement must either be genuinely cookieless and
first-party, or the policy changes first — in that order, never the reverse.
The cheapest honest option is a signup-source question in onboarding ("hoe hoorde
je van ons?"), which is first-party, needs no consent banner, and answers the
channel question directly.
