# Acquisition channels — Bullseye

Framework: Weinberg & Mares, *Traction*. Their central claim, quoting Thiel:
*"It is very likely that one channel is optimal. Most businesses actually get
zero distribution channels to work. Poor distribution — not product — is the
number one cause of failure."*

Bullseye is five steps: **brainstorm → rank → prioritise → test → focus**. The
discipline that matters is brainstorming *every* channel before ranking, because
we all arrive with biases. Ours is obvious and worth naming: this is a software
project, so the instinct is SEO and content. That instinct may be wrong for the
primary persona.

> **⭐ Re-ranked 2026-08-17 for the persona change.** This file was ranked for
> **Marijke, the heritage learner**. The primary persona became **Robin, the
> partner** on 2026-08-16 (`personas.md` §1). A channel ranking is downstream of
> who you are trying to reach, so the old inner ring did not survive the change
> unaltered — Marijke is reached by community and word of mouth and *does not
> search*, while Robin is **partly searchable**: mixed-nationality couple
> communities, Indonesian diaspora groups in NL, r/indonesia, wedding and visa
> forums. Search and existing platforms move up; heritage-community work moves
> to the middle ring, where it still serves Marijke.
>
> Nothing here has been tested. See § The measurement gap — it still blocks
> everything, and it blocks it harder now that two rankings disagree.

## The ranking

Against the 19 channels, ranked for **Robin, the partner** (primary since
2026-08-16). Marijke's ranking is preserved below, because if the first paying
customers turn out to be heritage learners the correct move is to revert, and
`personas.md` § "What would falsify these" names that as the newest and
least-tested claim in the file.

### Inner ring — test these three first

**1. Existing platforms + community, where couples actually are.** Robin's
communities are different from Marijke's and easier to reach without a phone
call: Indonesian diaspora groups in NL, mixed-nationality couple communities,
r/indonesia, and — the sharpest of them — visa and wedding forums, where someone
is already in the middle of an administrative process that makes the family
relationship concrete and urgent. Weinberg's caution applies: these are
communities, so being spammy fails immediately. Participate as the person who
built the thing, not as a campaign.
*Cheap test:* answer language questions genuinely in three such communities for
four weeks, with the product in the profile rather than in the posts. Measure
replies and profile clicks, not signups.

**2. Content marketing / SEO.** Promoted from third. Robin *does* search — for
"Indonesisch leren", for how to greet in-laws, for what *capek* means after
hearing it at dinner — where Marijke never thought of herself as a language
learner at all. The corpus already exists (affixes, grammar patterns, loanwords,
stories), there is no Dutch-language competition for these queries, and the
by-product pages are useful whether or not anyone buys.
*Cheap test:* publish the public loanword page and three affix explainers,
measure impressions in Search Console over six weeks.

**3. Targeting blogs / niche press.** Unchanged in rank and still strong, but the
pitch changes with the persona. The loanword story — *"173 Nederlandse woorden
die je al in het Indonesisch kent"* — remains genuinely good and placeable
independent of the product. For Robin's outlets, the partner story is the angle:
learning your partner's language as an adult, and the politeness that quietly
excludes you at the family table.
*Cheap test:* pitch five outlets — a mix of Indisch-heritage and
relationship/expat-life angles — and count placements.

### Middle ring — plausible, not first

- **Community building, heritage.** Indische and Moluccan organisations, *tempo
  doeloe* Facebook groups, pasar malam events, the Moluccan community in
  Vught/Assen/Capelle. These groups have newsletters, committees and event
  calendars, and an active interest in keeping the language alive. **This was the
  inner ring's first entry under Marijke and remains the highest-fit channel for
  her** — it moved down only because she is no longer primary, not because it got
  worse. If the falsification test in `personas.md` fires, this goes straight
  back to number one.
  *Cheap test, unchanged:* approach three organisations offering a talk ("de
  Nederlandse woorden in het Indonesisch"). Measure replies, not signups.
- **Viral marketing.** The loanword list is genuinely shareable — "did you know
  *kulkas* is *koelkast*?" is a WhatsApp-family-group message. Stronger under
  Robin than under Marijke, because the obvious person to send it to is the
  Indonesian partner, who will enjoy it and forward it. Not a strategy on its
  own, but the *product* should be built so sharing is easy.
- **Public relations.** The founder story (Dutch-Indonesian family, built the
  thing he wanted) is a real story, and regional press likes local-founder
  angles. Note it is now also the landing page's hero (D2), so the press angle
  and the site say the same thing — which is good, and means neither can drift
  without the other.
- **Speaking engagements.** Heritage association meetings, library talks. High
  fit with Marijke's demographic, poor scalability, but excellent for the
  customer-development conversations the pricing work needs — and those
  conversations are the thing that would let Dunford's step 1 finally happen.

### Outer ring — deprioritise for now

SEM (tiny search volume in Dutch for this pair, though cheap to test later),
social & display ads (no brand, weak targeting for heritage), offline ads,
email marketing (no list yet — becomes relevant once there is one), engineering
as marketing (the loanword page arguably *is* this), business development,
sales, affiliate programs, trade shows, offline events, unconventional PR.

Email is worth flagging as **deferred, not dismissed**: once there are a few
hundred learners, it is usually one of the strongest retention channels, and
retention is where a €7–9/month business actually lives.

## Why the ordering runs against instinct — and why that changed

Under Marijke the ordering ran hard against instinct. The temptation is to start
with SEO because it is the one we can do alone, at a keyboard, without talking to
anyone, and Weinberg's warning is precisely this: founders pick channels they are
comfortable with rather than channels their customers are in. Marijke does not
search for "leer Indonesisch" — she has never thought of herself as a language
learner. She hears about it from someone in her community.

**Robin makes the comfortable answer partly correct, which is a trap worth
naming.** He does search, so content earns its inner-ring place on the merits.
But "partly searchable" is not "searchable", and the half of Robin that lives in
couple and diaspora communities is still reached by showing up. If the ranking
ever collapses to *content only*, that is the bias reasserting itself under cover
of a persona change — check it against `personas.md` §1 before believing it.

## What to measure

Per *Traction*'s testing chapter, each test needs a number decided in advance.

| Test | Metric | Threshold to pursue |
|---|---|---|
| Four weeks participating in three couple/diaspora communities | replies + profile clicks | ≥20 genuine replies, ≥1 unprompted question about the product |
| Loanword page + 3 explainers | Search Console impressions at 6 weeks | ≥500/month and rising |
| Five blog/podcast pitches | placements | ≥1 placement |
| Three heritage organisations *(Marijke, middle ring)* | replies, and whether any offers a slot | ≥1 concrete opportunity |

None of these needs budget. All of them need a person, which is the actual
constraint — and the community test needs that person for four consecutive
weeks, which makes it the most expensive cheap test on the list.

## The measurement gap that blocks all of this

There is **no analytics on the public site** — no signup-source attribution, no
event tracking. Every conclusion above would currently be unfalsifiable.

Note the constraint this must respect: the privacy policy states there are no
analytics or tracking tools and no third-party cookies, and that claim is now
pinned by a test. Any measurement must either be genuinely cookieless and
first-party, or the policy changes first — in that order, never the reverse.
The cheapest honest option is a signup-source question in onboarding ("hoe hoorde
je van ons?"), which is first-party, needs no consent banner, and answers the
channel question directly.
