# StoryBrand — whose story is this?

Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand 2.0*. Book in `~/Downloads`.

The single most useful lens for this product, because its landing page tells a
founder's story and the trap is letting the founder be the hero.

---

## The premise

Miller's claim: people are drawn to brands that help them *survive and thrive*,
and they will not spend calories decoding a message. So the message must be
filtered into story shape, because story is the format humans process for free.

His story-in-a-nutshell, verbatim:

> A character who wants something encounters a problem before they can get it. At
> the peak of their despair, a guide steps into their lives, gives them a plan,
> and calls them to action. That action helps them avoid failure and experience a
> success.

## The seven parts

1. **A character** — the CUSTOMER, never you. They want something.
2. **has a problem** — external (the visible obstacle), internal (how it makes
   them feel), and philosophical (why it is *wrong* that this is hard).
3. **and meets a guide** — you. Two credentials only: **empathy** and **authority**.
4. **who gives them a plan** — the small number of steps that make acting feel safe.
5. **and calls them to action** — direct, not implied.
6. **that helps them avoid failure** — the stakes if nothing changes.
7. **and ends in success** — what their life looks like afterwards.

## Why this matters here specifically

The landing page opens on the owner's own experience. That is a *deliberate*
choice — `personas.md` §1 says the reason the partner persona is primary is that
it is the owner's own path, "which is the reason the landing page can drop the
marketing voice and tell it in the first person."

That is compatible with Miller, but only if the founder story is doing **guide**
work rather than **hero** work:

- **Guide work:** "I have been where you are" (empathy) + "and I found the way
  through" (authority) → *then it turns to the reader.*
- **Hero work:** the story ends with what the founder built, and the reader is
  left admiring rather than recognising.

**The test is the last sentence of the hero.** If it ends in the builder's life —
*"Dus heb ik het als één ding gebouwd"* — it is a memoir. If it ends in the
reader's — *"…zodat jij aan tafel iets terug kunt zeggen"* — the guide has
handed over.

## The three problem layers, applied

Miller's point is that most copy addresses only the external problem, while
people actually buy to resolve the internal one.

| Layer | For Robin |
|---|---|
| **External** | He cannot speak or follow Indonesian |
| **Internal** | He is the person the room accommodates — politely excluded at his partner's family table |
| **Philosophical** | Nobody should need a second hobby, or a server, to learn the language of the person they live with |

The internal one is the reason he would pay. `personas.md` §1 states it directly:
*"They want to stop being the person the room accommodates."* Copy that only
addresses the external problem sells a course; copy that names the internal one
sells belonging.

⚠️ The philosophical layer is where over-claiming creeps in. "Nobody should have
to build a server to learn a language" is fair. "Language learning should be
effortless" is not, and the register limit forbids it.

## The grunt test

> Could a caveman look at your website and immediately grunt your offer back to
> you?

Three answers must be available in about five seconds:
1. What do you offer?
2. How will it make my life better?
3. What do I do next?

Run this on the rendered page at 1440×900 **and** at 390×844, not on the copy
file. It is a layout test as much as a writing test — and on this page the answer
to (1) currently lives in a small gold eyebrow above the headline, which is worth
questioning.

## The one-liner

A single sentence, for the meta description, a reply in a forum, the answer to
"so what do you do?". Shape: **character + problem + plan + success.**

Draft for this product, as raw material rather than a finished line:

> Nederlanders met een Indonesische partner willen meepraten aan tafel, maar er
> bestaat geen fatsoenlijke cursus in het Nederlands. Kamoe Bisa is één cursus die
> al gevuld is — grammatica, verhalen en herhaling in één sessie per dag — zodat
> je niet langer degene bent voor wie iedereen overschakelt.

Note it never uses the word "app", never lists features, and ends in the reader's
life. That is the shape to keep even if every word changes.

## What Miller gets wrong for this case

His framework is built for businesses with customers, testimonials and a track
record — it leans on social proof for the authority credential, and this product
has **none** and may not invent any. So authority here has to come from the two
things that are real:

- **The founder's own path** — he did the thing, badly, for years.
- **The audit** — ADR 0007, the product's own measured mistake and what changed
  because of it. Miller would call that authority; it is also the most honest
  form available, because it is a credential built out of an admission.
