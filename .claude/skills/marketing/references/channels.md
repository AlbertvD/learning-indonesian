# Channels — choosing where to spend effort

From Weinberg & Mares, *Traction* (2014).
`docs/marketing/channels.md` already carries the live ranking — **read it first
and edit it there**; this file is the method behind it.

---

## The claim

Their central argument, quoting Thiel:

> It is very likely that one channel is optimal. Most businesses actually get
> zero distribution channels to work. Poor distribution — not product — is the
> number one cause of failure.

Hence: don't spread thin. Find the one channel that works right now, then wring
it dry. When it saturates, run the process again.

## The 19 channels

Brainstorm against all of them, not the ones that come to mind:

viral marketing · public relations · unconventional PR · search engine marketing ·
social & display ads · offline ads · SEO · content marketing · email marketing ·
engineering as marketing · targeting blogs · business development · sales ·
affiliate programs · existing platforms · trade shows · offline events ·
speaking engagements · community building

## Bullseye — five steps

1. **Brainstorm** — one plausible idea for *every* channel. This step exists to
   counteract bias; skipping channels here is the whole failure mode.
2. **Rank** — three columns: inner circle (promising now), potential, long-shot.
3. **Prioritise** — pick the top three. Draw the line where excitement drops off.
4. **Test** — cheap, parallel tests answering: what does a customer cost here,
   how many are available, and are they the customers we want *right now*?
   You are not trying to get traction yet, only to find out if the channel could
   work. "Run four Facebook ads, not forty."
5. **Focus** — pour everything into the one that worked, until it saturates.

Then repeat. Mint used exactly this: brainstormed, picked targeting blogs / PR /
SEM, ran cheap tests, focused on targeting blogs for the first 40,000 users, then
re-ran Bullseye and moved to PR for the next million.

## The bias trap, which is the reason this framework exists

> Most founders only consider traction channels they're already familiar with.

For a solo software project the bias is obvious and named in `channels.md:14-17`:
**SEO and content, because they can be done alone, at a keyboard, without talking
to anyone.** That is a comfort ranking, not a customer ranking.

The correction is to ask where the *persona* actually is, not where you are
willing to go. If the answer is "in a community you'd have to phone someone to
reach", that is the channel, and preferring SEO because it's introvert-friendly
is precisely the mistake Weinberg is warning about.

## Two disciplines that are easy to skip

**Decide the number before the test.** Every test needs a threshold set in
advance, or you will rationalise whatever happens. `channels.md` §"What to
measure" already does this — keep it that way when adding tests.

**Don't fall into the product trap.** Pursue traction and product development in
parallel, spending roughly equal time. The instinct — "I'll do marketing once the
product is ready" — is how products arrive finished and unheard-of.

## The constraint specific to this product

Two things bound every channel decision here, and both are in `channels.md`:

**There is no analytics on the public site.** No signup-source attribution, no
event tracking — so every ranking is currently unfalsifiable. Worse, the privacy
policy states there are no analytics, no tracking tools and no third-party
cookies, **and that claim is pinned by a test.** Any measurement must be
genuinely cookieless and first-party, or the policy changes first — in that
order, never the reverse. The cheapest honest option is a signup-source question
in onboarding ("hoe hoorde je van ons?").

**The constraint is a person, not a budget.** None of the inner-ring tests need
money. All of them need someone to make a phone call or write a pitch.

## When the persona changes, the ranking changes

This is not a detail. A channel ranking is downstream of *who you are trying to
reach*, so a persona change invalidates it. `personas.md` §"How the four change
what we build" gives each persona's channel row — start there, then re-rank.
